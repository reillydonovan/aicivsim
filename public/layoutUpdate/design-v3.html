Design v3 — audit & proposal

A full-site design and flow review of the AI Civilization Simulator, conducted the way a platform design team would run a design crit: what the product is trying to say, where the current design says it well, where it gets in its own way, and a working prototype of a stronger direction.

Branch design-v3 Reviewed August 14, 2026 Scope 15 pages, both themes, desktop + mobile
Open the prototype Current design

Executive summary

This site has a real point of view — a Feltron-inspired editorial data language that most dashboards never achieve — and content strong enough to carry it. The problems are not aesthetic. They are structural: every screen presents ten things with equal weight, so no screen is about anything. The navigation lists fifteen destinations in one flat row of 10-pixel links. The product's central interaction — choosing a scenario and watching civilization's trajectory change — is rendered as a row of tiny dot-buttons tucked into a control bar. Grids don't resolve (seven items in six columns), motion gates legibility instead of confirming action, and the type system uses one voice — small uppercase tracked labels — for five different levels of hierarchy.

The proposal keeps the brand (dark editorial, Space Grotesk / Inter / JetBrains Mono, the orange accent, the four scenario hues) and re-architects the presentation around one principle: one story per screen, and the scenario choice is the product.

What's working — keep it

The editorial identityDark, dense, confident. Distinctive against every SaaS-styled dashboard. The brand survives v3 intact.
The score narrative"Today's score → projected 2050" is the right spine for every page. It just needs typographic staging.
Scenario color systemFour fixed hues used consistently across charts, chips, and text. Rare discipline; preserve exactly.
Live-data groundingFetching NOAA / OWID / World Bank baselines client-side is a genuine credibility feature no competitor has.
The year sliderThe Simulation page's giant "2035" with a hairline scrubber is the best control on the site — a model for the rest.
The writing"Every number is a lever; every scenario a possible world." The copy is better than the layout it sits in.

Findings

01

Fifteen flat navigation items

High

The nav is a single row of 15 links at 10px with 6px padding — recently tightened just to fit one line at 1080px. That is the symptom speaking: the IA has outgrown a flat list. Links of entirely different kinds (six system dashboards, three tools, four reference pages, a chat agent) sit side by side with identical weight, so nothing communicates structure and everything communicates clutter.

Five top-level destinations — Overview · Systems ▾ · Tools ▾ · Evidence ▾ · About — with hover/focus menus that carry hints (today's score per system, "5 levers · 50 yr"). Bigger type, fewer decisions, the structure of the product visible in its chrome. Prototyped in index-v3.

02

The scenario switcher — the core interaction — looks like an afterthought

High

Four tiny dot-buttons, right-aligned in a secondary bar, active state distinguishable only by color at 10px. This is the single control that changes every number on every page — the product's entire thesis — and a first-time visitor can miss it completely. The homepage then compensates with a four-step "How to use this site" manual.

A first-class segmented control seated in the hero, directly under the score it changes. Switching tweens the score, repaints the projection, rewrites the outcome sentence — the site teaches itself in one click and the instruction manual disappears. Prototyped.

03

No focal hierarchy — every screen is ten equal things

High

The homepage presents the score block, a seven-card systems grid, a pullquote, three stat cells, a four-step guide, two stacked 3D banners, four scenario cards, a timeline teaser, and a methodology block — all at similar visual temperature. The eye has no landing sequence; scanning is work.

Each screen gets one protagonist (home: the score and the choice), one supporting section, and everything else recedes into links. The prototype homepage is three sections and a footer, and it says more than the current nine.

04

Grids that don't resolve

Medium

Seven system cards flow into a six-column grid, orphaning "Strategy" beside a large empty slab. "The Seven Systems" repeats the failure in two columns with a visible empty bordered cell. A layout that ends mid-thought reads as unfinished regardless of styling.

Content whose count can change lives in lists, not fixed grids. The prototype's systems ledger is a table that resolves at any row count — and gains a per-row today→2050 comparison the cards never had.

05

Scroll-reveal gates legibility

Medium

Sections sit at near-zero opacity until an IntersectionObserver fires — including content already in the viewport after anchor jumps and tab switches (a known quirk documented in the repo's own skills). Half the audit screenshots caught ghost text. Motion that makes users wait to read is decoration at the content's expense.

Motion confirms, never gates: numbers tween when the user acts, bars re-fill on scenario change, and content is always at full opacity. If entrance animation is kept, start at 60%+ opacity with an 8px rise, 200ms, triggered one viewport early — and honor prefers-reduced-motion.

06

One typographic voice doing five jobs

Medium

Section numbers, section titles, card labels, tab labels, and metadata all use the same ~10px uppercase letterspaced treatment. When everything whispers in the same voice, hierarchy flattens and density stops being a style and becomes a tax. Body text at 13px over 1080px measures is below comfortable reading size.

A real scale with real jumps — 11 eyebrow / 13.5 caption / 15 body / 17 lead / 19–28 titles / display numerals — sentence case for headings, caps-tracking reserved for exactly one tier (the eyebrow). Tokenized in the prototype's CSS.

07

Accent color means four different things

Medium

Orange (#d4622a) is simultaneously the brand accent, the "worst case" scenario hue, the danger color for declining scores, and the link color. Cyan doubles as "aggressive scenario" and ad-hoc highlight for lever values; blue appears un-systematically for adoption stats. The palette has discipline in charts and none in UI.

One rule: orange is for interaction (links, buttons, active states); scenario hues appear only on scenario identity (segments, chart series, projected values); semantic red/green only on deltas. Where "worst case" and the accent collide, the scenario context wins.

08

Two stacked control bars with drifting layouts

Medium

Every content page carries a site-nav row plus a sticky control bar of page tabs and scenario dots — six tabs and four scenario buttons share one row on Climate, alignment differing page to page. Two rows of chrome before content, with the global control (scenario) visually subordinate to local ones (tabs).

One bar: navigation. Scenario control lives with the content it changes (hero-seated, as prototyped). Page sections become an on-page contents rail or plain anchor sections — Apple product pages don't tab; they scroll.

09

Vertical rhythm swings from cramped to void

Low

Timeline entries float in ~400px of empty column; the homepage ends in a near-empty viewport before a one-line footer; elsewhere score blocks sit 24px from unrelated sections. Whitespace reads as intent only when it's consistent.

Fixed rhythm on an 8px base — 96px between sections, 40px head-to-content, 24px inside cards — and a real footer (wordmark, link columns, data credits) so pages end on purpose. Prototyped.

10

Copy and chrome drift

Low

The hero says "six operating systems"; the homepage lists seven cards; a section is titled "The Seven Systems"; About says six domains. Page H1s ("The World, Right Now") diverge from nav labels ("Live Data") without a connecting pattern. Chart legends repeat four scenario entries under every single chart — eight times on Climate alone.

Decide the number (six systems + the Simulation engine as a tool is the honest count), align hero/nav/About, and hoist chart legends to one shared legend per section. Small edits, outsized credibility gain.

Design principles for v3

1
One story per screenEvery screen has a protagonist. Everything else supports it or leaves.
2
The scenario is the productThe choice between futures is the thesis — give it the best control on the site, seated where its consequences are visible.
3
Layouts resolveNo orphan cells, no accidental voids. Lists for variable counts, grids only for fixed ones.
4
Hierarchy through scale, not capsReal type jumps, sentence case, tracking reserved for one tier. Density stays; strain goes.
5
Motion confirms, never gatesAnimate consequences of user action. Content is always readable. Reduced-motion respected.
6
Show, don't instructIf the interface needs a usage manual on the homepage, the interface is the bug.

The prototype

index-v3.html is the proposal, working. What to test:

  • Switch scenarios in the hero segmented control — score, delta bar, outcome sentence, all six ledger rows, and the quartet cards update together, with motion only on the numbers you changed.
  • Hover the nav groups — Systems/Tools/Evidence menus with per-item hints replace thirteen naked links.
  • The systems ledger — seven rows, no orphans, each row a complete today→2050 sentence.
  • Toggle the theme (nav, far right) — both themes share the token system; the choice persists to the live site's key.
  • Resize below 900px — the ledger reflows to a two-line row; the quartet goes 2-up; nothing wraps into a second nav row.

It reads the live site's aicivsim-scenario and aicivsim-theme localStorage keys and hash (#aggressive…), so it drops into the existing persistence model unchanged.
